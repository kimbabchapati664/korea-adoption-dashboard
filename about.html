About me

Hi, I'm HJ Kim.

"Whatever your story is, I'm happy to help with my time."

I was born and grew up in Korea. In my 20s and 30s I had the privilege of living in other countries: Madagascar, Singapore, France, and India in my 30s.

I moved back to Korea ten years ago. Recently I moved again, to my hometown near Busan, the place I originally came from and where all my family and my ancestors came from. It is pretty thrilling to come back here and make a new family. I moved back with my German husband.

As a Korean, I had a mom and a dad. But I also lived through the kind of life stories that came from how much Korea changed after the Korean War. Each one of us has a story.

In my 20s I started volunteering with different NGOs, groups and committees in Korea that helped Korean adoptees look for their birth parents. Back then most of the adoptees I met were my generation, my age. We were all in our 20s, all struggling to find out who we were, with a lot of anger and anxiety.

Now, in 2026, many things have changed in the world and in Korea. I'm glad Korea has become a more inclusive country. We have opened up to many people, and I think it is a very good time to find your roots. Whatever your story, whatever information you have, or anything in between, I'm happy to help with my time.

How I work

Three promises

Free comes first

Your records request, your file review and your police DNA registration are free through NCRC and the Korean police. I will always point you there first. I will never sell you access to your own file.

Honest about the odds

Many files are thin, and some are wrong. I will tell you plainly what your paperwork does and does not make possible, before you spend anything or get your hopes up.

Your story stays yours

Nothing identifiable about you or your birth family goes on this site or anywhere else without your explicit permission.

Where I can help

As a volunteer, when time allows

Reading your papers

Going through Korean documents with you and explaining what they actually say.

Calls in Korean

Phoning NCRC, an agency, a hospital or a children's home when English is not getting you anywhere.

Your first request

Helping you prepare the Adoption Information Disclosure Request so nothing is missed.

The right people

Pointing you to the adoptee-led groups and services that fit your situation.
